# Customer Cohort & Retention Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer purchasing behavior over time using cohort analysis. Customers are grouped based on the month of their first purchase, allowing retention and repeat purchasing behavior to be tracked across subsequent months.

Python was used for data cleaning, cohort creation, retention calculations, and exploratory analysis. Tableau was then used to build interactive dashboards presenting customer retention, acquisition trends, revenue, and repeat customer value.

---

## Business Questions

This project aims to answer the following questions:

1. How well are customers retained after their first purchase?
2. How does customer retention change as cohorts age?
3. How has new customer acquisition changed over time?
4. How much repeat revenue is generated by customers after acquisition?
5. Which customer cohorts generate the highest repeat revenue per customer?
6. Are changes in overall revenue driven by customer acquisition or post-acquisition customer value?

## Analysis Process

### 1. Data Cleaning

The dataset was inspected for:

- Missing values
- Duplicate records
- Blank values
- Incorrect data types
- Invalid numerical values
- Transaction date range

Transaction Date was converted to datetime format before performing time-based analysis.

### 2. Customer Cohort Creation

Each customer's first purchase date was identified.

Customers were then assigned to a **Cohort Month**, representing the month in which they made their first purchase.

A **Transaction Month** was also created for every transaction.

### 3. Cohort Index

A Cohort Index was calculated to measure the number of months between the customer's first purchase month and each subsequent transaction.

- Cohort Index 0 = acquisition month
- Cohort Index 1 = one month after acquisition
- Cohort Index 2 = two months after acquisition
- etc.

### 4. Retention Analysis

Customers were grouped by Cohort Month and Cohort Index.

Monthly retention was calculated by comparing the number of active customers in each subsequent month with the original number of customers in that cohort.

This was visualized using a cohort retention heatmap.

### 5. Repeat Revenue Analysis

Repeat purchasing behavior was analyzed separately from the initial acquisition month.

A standardized 3-month window (Cohort Index 1–3) was used to compare repeat revenue across cohorts.

Repeat revenue per customer was also calculated so that cohorts of different sizes could be compared more fairly.

## Dashboard 1 – Customer Cohort & Retention Analysis

![Customer Cohort and Retention Analysis](Customer%20Cohort%20and%20Retention%20Analysis.png)

The main dashboard provides an overview of:

- Total Revenue
- Unique Customers
- Repeat Customers
- Repeat Customer Rate
- Monthly Customer Retention
- Monthly Revenue Trend

### Key Findings

- Total revenue reached approximately **$245.95M**.
- The dataset contains approximately **84.6K unique customers**.
- Approximately **56.9K customers made repeat purchases**.
- The overall repeat customer rate was approximately **67.3%**.
- Monthly cohort retention after the acquisition month generally remained around **7–9%**.
- Overall monthly revenue remained relatively stable despite changes in customer acquisition.

---

## Dashboard 2 – Customer Cohort Value Analysis

![Customer Cohort Value Analysis](Customer%20Cohort%20Value%20Analysis.png)

The second dashboard focuses on comparing customer cohorts using:

- Cohort Size by Acquisition Month
- 3-Month Repeat Revenue per Customer

### Key Findings

Customer acquisition declined considerably across later cohorts.

However, 3-month repeat revenue per customer remained relatively stable across most cohorts, generally around **$290–$350 per customer**.

This suggests that the decline in cohort size was more significant than changes in post-acquisition customer value. Maintaining or improving customer acquisition may therefore be an important business opportunity.
